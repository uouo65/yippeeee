Cross-species brain grafts: not conclusions, but a navigator for what comes next

Experiments that transplant human brain cells into other species raise ethical questions, yet researchers say the insights are hard to obtain any other way.

M
Meridian Science Desk
Updated Nov 23, 2026, 9:00 AM

While this research primarily draws implications without clear conclusions, this does not mean that this experiment was pointless. Through the grafts, diseases especially related to human brain cells, such as certain types of dementia, Autism, and Schizophrenia, could be examined in other species. Some may oppose this idea, considering that ethical issues remain and suggest computer simulation as an alternative. But conducting such experiments still requires a massive amount of data, meaning these kinds of experiments are indispensable. It is true for now that the grafts remain immature and have clear restraints, as they have not grown and formed connections within their original host, humans. But valuable insights—and even some information that can only be obtained from a non-human context by inter-species comparison—still exist. Thus, we can't say that it is useless; rather, it is a navigator to significant breakthroughs.
